More and more people ask an AI assistant directly instead of searching Google: "Which company would you recommend to build an app?". The answer mentions a handful of brands. **Generative engine optimization (GEO)** is the work required to make yours one of them.

## How AI assistants choose their sources

Tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews combine two sources of information: what they learned during training and what they find on the web in real time. In both cases they favour pages that:

- Can be **crawled and indexed** without issues.
- Answer a question **directly and specifically**.
- Describe the company **consistently** across different sources.
- Have **authority**: other trusted sites mention or link to them.

## 1. Let AI crawlers in

Check your `robots.txt`. If you block agents such as `OAI-SearchBot`, `PerplexityBot` or `Claude-SearchBot`, your site cannot appear in those assistants' search-backed answers. Also make sure your CDN or firewall isn't blocking them.

## 2. Write answers, not just sales copy

Start each service page with a 60–90 word paragraph answering "what is it" and "who is it for". Add real FAQs, comparison tables and concrete facts. That is the format models quote most easily.

## 3. Use consistent structured data

Mark up your site with schema.org: `Organization` for the company, `Service` for each service, `FAQPage` for FAQs and `BreadcrumbList` for navigation. The markup must match what is visible on the page.

## 4. Define your brand entity

Always use the same name, description, services and contact details on your website, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile and industry directories. Models cross-check these sources to decide what your company is and whether it is trustworthy.

## 5. Measure visibility

Regularly ask several assistants about your service category and note whether you appear, in which position and with which sources. In Google Search Console, AI Overviews traffic is included in the web search performance report.
