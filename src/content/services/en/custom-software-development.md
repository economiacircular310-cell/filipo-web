## When does custom software make sense?

A generic tool is enough as long as your processes are standard. Custom software starts paying off when:

- Your team loses hours to manual tasks, spreadsheets or copying data between tools.
- You pay for several SaaS subscriptions that don't talk to each other.
- The way you work is a competitive advantage and no off-the-shelf tool reflects it.
- You need to offer your own digital product to your customers.

## Custom software vs. generic solutions

| | Custom software | Generic SaaS |

| Ownership of code and data | Yours | The vendor's |
| Long-term cost | Upfront investment, no per-seat licences | Recurring fees that grow with your team |
| Integrations | Whatever you need | Whatever the vendor offers |
